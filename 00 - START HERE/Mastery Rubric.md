# Mastery Rubric (0–5)

Mastery measures what you can **do** without help. Reading a note never raises your level.

| Level | Name | You can… | Evidence required before you record this level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Not started | — | — |
| **1** | Recognise | Define the key terms and explain the basic idea in your own words | Flashcards for the topic ≥ 80% correct from memory |
| **2** | Guided | Solve the worked examples while looking at hints or notes | Re-derived Examples 1–2 with the note open |
| **3** | Independent | Solve standard (L1–L3) problems **without hints** | L1–L3 practice correct, closed book; self-test ≥ 80% |
| **4** | Transfer | Solve unfamiliar (L4) problems and **explain why** each step is valid | One L4 problem solved and explained aloud or in writing; blank-page recall covers ≥ 80% of the summary |
| **5** | Apply and defend | Use the concept in a project or lab and defend design decisions against challenge | A project/lab artifact that uses the concept, plus answers to "why not X instead?" (L5 problem or viva question) |

## Rules

1. **Evidence before entry.** Raise `mastery:` in a lesson's frontmatter only once the evidence above exists, and keep the evidence (solution files, lab notes) in the vault.
2. **Decay is real.** If a scheduled review scores below 50%, drop the level by one and restart the review cycle ([[Review Schedule]]).
3. **"Mastered" (`status: mastered`)** requires level ≥ 4 *and* a passed 14-day review.
4. **Exam readiness for a module** = every `provisional-core` topic at level ≥ 3, and ≥ 70% of them at level ≥ 4.
5. Log every mistake you make on practice or mock exams in [[Mistake Log]]. A recurring mistake pattern is a weak area ([[Weak Area Tracker]]).
